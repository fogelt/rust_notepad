# PewDiePie Fends Off Drog in Swedish Forest, Emerges With Only a Broken Headset

**JÖNKÖPING, Sweden** — Felix Kjellberg, better known as PewDiePie, was walking home from a friend's cabin on Tuesday evening when he encountered a drog in the pine forest and, according to everyone present, handled it with surprising calm.

The drog — a 60-kilogram, antlered bog-dweller from Swedish folklore that most people assume went extinct sometime in the 1800s — reportedly blocked the path near a wooden footbridge and refused to move for eleven minutes.

"I thought it was a very large dog until it stood up," Kjellberg said in a video posted Wednesday. "Then I thought it was a very large problem."

## What happened

The encounter began at 19:42, about two kilometres from the cabin. Kjellberg was carrying a thermos, a camera bag and a pair of wireless headphones. He put the headphones on, turned the volume up, and attempted to walk around the animal. The drog followed.

According to Kjellberg, the situation escalated when the drog knocked the thermos out of his hand with its snout. He then did what he described as "the only sensible thing": he took off his jacket, waved it above his head, and made a noise he compared to "a disappointed goose."

The drog stopped. It tilted its head. It made a sound that three separate witnesses described as "rude."

## The eleven minutes

- **19:42** — Drog blocks the footbridge. Kjellberg assumes it is a dog.
- **19:44** — Drog stands up. Assumption revised.
- **19:46** — Thermos knocked into the stream. Kjellberg begins negotiations.
- **19:49** — Jacket removed and waved. Drog appears unimpressed but curious.
- **19:53** — Drog circles the bridge twice, then sits down in the middle of it.
- **19:57** — Kjellberg plays a video at full volume. Drog watches the entire thing.
- **20:01** — Drog walks into the marsh and disappears. Kjellberg retrieves the thermos.

## A local expert weighs in

Dr. Linnéa Holm, a folklorist at Jönköping University who has studied drog sightings for nine years, said the animal's behaviour was consistent with a young adult male defending a bridge.

"Drogs are territorial, dramatic and easily bored," Holm said. "Standing your ground and making yourself look ridiculous is actually the recommended response. Waving a jacket is textbook. The goose noise is optional."

Holm added that the drog population in the region has been quietly recovering since the 1990s, and that the animal is protected under Swedish law. Interfering with a drog carries a fine of 4,000 kronor, which is why Kjellberg says he did not throw anything at it.

## Witness account

"It was very polite for a monster," said Marta Lindqvist, 68, who watched from her kitchen window with a cup of tea. "He talked to it the whole time. I don't think it understood a word, but neither did I, and I stayed to the end."

Her husband, Bengt, described the scene as "the most Swedish thing I have ever seen," adding that he has lived in the area for forty years and has never seen a drog, "except once, and nobody believes me."

## What happens next

Kjellberg says he plans to return to the cabin next month, this time with a flashlight and "a better plan." He has not replaced the headset. The thermos was recovered, dented, and is now displayed on a shelf in his studio.

The Swedish Environmental Protection Agency said it was aware of the sighting and asked the public to keep a respectful distance from drog habitat, especially near bridges.

A spokesperson added: "Do not wave your jacket at a drog. Unless it works. Then, by all means."
